Some cups send the top teams of each group through, then fill the last places with the best third-placed teams. Third-placed teams never met, and their groups may not be the same size, so a plain look at the tables is not fair. Seazn compares them on the same number of matches.

## Why the thirds are hard to compare

Two teams from different groups have no match between them, so there is no head-to-head result to look at. That step is left out of the comparison. What is left is the record each team made in its own group.

The groups may also differ in size. {{fact:best-third-placed-teams.teams}} teams cannot be split into {{fact:best-third-placed-teams.groupCount}} equal groups, so a group has an extra team, and its third-placed team has played one more match than the others. More matches means more chances to score points, and that is not a better team.

## The same number of matches

The smallest group sets the number: every third-placed team is compared on {{fact:best-third-placed-teams.comparedMatches}} matches. A group with more teams than the smallest has its bottom teams' results left out of its third-placed team's record, one team for each extra team in the group. {{fact:best-third-placed-teams.largerGroup}} has {{fact:best-third-placed-teams.largerGroupSize}} teams and the smallest group has {{fact:best-third-placed-teams.smallestGroupSize}}, so the results against its last-placed team are left out. That team is {{fact:best-third-placed-teams.leftOutTeam}}, who finished {{fact:best-third-placed-teams.leftOutPlace}}.

{{fact:best-third-placed-teams.largerThird}} finished third in their group with {{fact:best-third-placed-teams.largerThirdFullRecord}}. Their win over {{fact:best-third-placed-teams.leftOutTeam}} gave all of those points. In the smaller groups third place is last place, so there is nobody beneath the third-placed team to take points from. Without that match their record is {{fact:best-third-placed-teams.largerThirdComparedRecord}}, which is what the other two teams are measured against. A group as small as the smallest has nothing left out, and when every group is the same size nothing is left out anywhere.

## Counting every result gives another answer

With every result counted, {{fact:best-third-placed-teams.fullRecordLeader}} would lead the third-placed teams. On the same number of matches the first is {{fact:best-third-placed-teams.comparedLeader}}. That is the point of the rule: the extra team in a group is the one a third-placed team can score off, and the others have no such team.

So the third-placed teams that go through are {{fact:best-third-placed-teams.thirdsThrough}}, and {{fact:best-third-placed-teams.outThird}} is out.

## How they are ranked

The compared records are put in order by the sport's own table rules, with the head-to-head steps removed. In football that is points first, then goal difference. [Two teams finish level on points: who finishes higher?](/blog/level-on-points-who-finishes-higher) covers how a table treats teams that are level on points.

## What Seazn writes down

Seazn writes the comparison down under the group tables, so nobody has to work it out. The line is "{{fact:best-third-placed-teams.footnoteLine}}". A "{{fact:best-third-placed-teams.leftOutButton}}" button opens the list of results that were left out: "{{fact:best-third-placed-teams.footnoteLeftOutLine}}". A group that left nothing out says so too: "{{fact:best-third-placed-teams.footnoteNothingLeftOut}}". Where [the groups feed the knockout](/blog/groups-into-knockout), these third-placed teams take the seats after the group winners and runners-up.

## In every sport

Groups with the best third-placed teams are offered in all {{fact:best-third-placed-teams.sportCount}} sports. Each sport compares the third-placed teams by its own table order, with the head-to-head steps removed, and what changes from sport to sport is which records come first, never how many matches they are compared on.
