In a Swiss, nobody plays everybody, so two players who finish on the same points may have met completely different opposition, and quite often they never met each other. Head-to-head, the usual way to separate two players, has nothing to look at. What the table can look at is how strong the people they did play turned out to be. That is what Buchholz and Sonneborn-Berger measure, and they are why a Swiss table can put one of two level players above the other without a rematch.

## The three figures

**Buchholz** is the sum of the points your opponents finished on. Play strong opponents and the figure is high; play weak ones and it is low. **Buchholz Cut-1** is the same sum with the lowest opponent's score left out, so a single very weak opponent does not drag a player down. **Sonneborn-Berger** adds up the scores of the opponents you beat, and half the scores of the opponents you drew with. It cares about who you beat, not only who you played. The sums follow the FIDE tie-break rules, and the table shows them in the same points as the results: a win is one, a draw is a half, and a loss is nothing.

## A round with no game

Nobody has to be paired against anyone in every round: with an odd number of players somebody sits out and is given a bye. A bye has no opponent whose score could be added up, so the tie-break reads it as a game against an imaginary opponent who finishes on the player's own points, but never on more than a draw is worth in every round: with {{fact:buchholz-bye.rounds}} rounds that is at most {{fact:buchholz-bye.dummyCap}} points (FIDE C.07, article 16.4). The bye itself counts as a win, the result its points match (also article 16.4). The second table is {{fact:buchholz-bye.players}} players over {{fact:buchholz-bye.rounds}} rounds, with a bye for one of them in each round.

Cy and Dev both had a bye and both finish on {{fact:buchholz-bye.cyPoints}} points. The bye is worth the same to each of them, their own points, which is exactly the most it can be worth, so the real games decide. Cy's games read: {{fact:buchholz-bye.cyGames}}. Dev's read: {{fact:buchholz-bye.devGames}}. Cy's Buchholz Cut-1 is {{fact:buchholz-bye.cyCut1}} against {{fact:buchholz-bye.devCut1}} for Dev, so Cy finishes {{fact:buchholz-bye.cyPlace}} and Dev {{fact:buchholz-bye.devPlace}}. {{fact:buchholz-bye.cappedPlayer}} had a bye as well and finishes on {{fact:buchholz-bye.cappedPoints}} points, more than the cap. The imaginary opponent therefore counts for {{fact:buchholz-bye.dummyCap}}, not {{fact:buchholz-bye.cappedPoints}}: {{fact:buchholz-bye.cappedPlayer}}'s Buchholz is {{fact:buchholz-bye.cappedBuchholz}}, where counting the bye at the player's own points would give {{fact:buchholz-bye.uncappedBuchholz}}. Leave the bye out of the sum altogether and the Buchholz of everyone who had one would be lower, which is why the table does not do it.

## Where you set it

The division's **Standings** tab prints the order under the table as "Tie-break cascade", and a chess Swiss reads points, then {{fact:buchholz-sonneborn-berger.boardgameSwissOrder}}. The Swiss table has a column for each figure, and beside two players who were separated it says "Tied with" and the other player, and "split on" followed by the figure: for example "split on Buchholz Cut-1". The stage's **Tie-breaks** button on the **Fixtures** tab edits the list until the stage starts.
