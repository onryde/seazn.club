The top teams of each group go through, and they are seeded by how they finished: every group winner first, then every runner-up. In the knockout the best seed meets the worst, so winners meet runners-up. And Seazn makes sure that two teams from the same group do not meet in round one.

## Dealing the groups

The teams are dealt into groups by seed, along the groups and back again: the best seeds lead the groups in order, the next seeds go back along them, then forward again. That keeps the groups as even as they can be. Here are {{fact:groups-into-knockout.teams}} football teams, listed best seed first: {{fact:groups-into-knockout.signUpOrder}}. They make {{fact:groups-into-knockout.groupCount}} groups of {{fact:groups-into-knockout.groupSize}}, and each group plays a round robin, which [the round-robin schedule post](/blog/round-robin-schedule) explains.

## Who goes through, and in what order

The top {{fact:groups-into-knockout.advance}} of each group go through, which is {{fact:groups-into-knockout.qualifiers}} teams. They are seated in order: the winners of every group first, then the runners-up. The picture marks them in each group.

Two group winners have never met, so there is no match between them to compare. They are ranked by the sport's table rules without the head-to-head step. In football that is points first, then goal difference, as the table's Finished column shows. The same goes for the runners-up.

## The bracket

A knockout holds a power of two places. {{fact:groups-into-knockout.qualifiers}} teams in {{fact:groups-into-knockout.bracketSize}} places leave {{fact:groups-into-knockout.byes}} empty, and the empty places go to the best seeds, who are straight through to the next round: {{fact:groups-into-knockout.straightThrough}}. In each match the two seeds add up to one more than the number of places, so the best seed meets the last place, the next best the one before it, and so on. The best of the rest meets the worst of them, and a group winner meets another group's runner-up.

## Keeping a group apart in round one

By the table alone, round one would pit {{fact:groups-into-knockout.tableOrderMatch}}, two teams from {{fact:groups-into-knockout.tableOrderClashGroup}}. They already met in the group, and a knockout should not open with a rematch. So the seats are arranged. The group winners keep theirs. The runners-up are tried in the order of the table, and the first arrangement in which no group meets itself is the one used. Here {{fact:groups-into-knockout.movedUp}} goes up a seat and {{fact:groups-into-knockout.movedDown}} goes down one, and round one is {{fact:groups-into-knockout.drawnMatches}}.

You see this before it is final. When every group is done, the fixtures page says "{{fact:groups-into-knockout.proposalBadge}}". Under "{{fact:groups-into-knockout.proposalHeading}}" it lists each team with the group place it came from and the seat it takes, and the bracket carries a note: "{{fact:groups-into-knockout.drawNote}}" Nothing is filled in until the organiser presses "{{fact:groups-into-knockout.confirmButton}}". {{fact:groups-into-knockout.confirmFinal}}

Often the table's own order is already enough. With four groups and two through from each, round one is {{fact:groups-into-knockout.fourGroupsRoundOne}}. The letter is the group, and the number is the place in it: A1 is the winner of group A and B2 is the runner-up of group B. No group meets itself, so nothing moves.

If no arrangement keeps every group apart, Seazn does not seat the bracket, and says so. It never draws a first round that pairs a group with itself.

## In every sport

Groups into a knockout are offered in all {{fact:groups-into-knockout.sportCount}} sports. Each sport ranks the teams in a group by its own table rules, so what changes from sport to sport is who finishes where, and never how the groups feed the bracket.
