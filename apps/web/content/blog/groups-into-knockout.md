The top teams of each group go through, and they are seated by the place they finished in: every group winner first, then every runner-up, each in the order of the groups. In the knockout the best seat meets the worst, so a group winner mostly meets a runner-up from another group. And Seazn makes sure that two teams from the same group do not meet in round one.

## Dealing the groups

The teams are dealt into groups by seed, along the groups and back again: the best seeds lead the groups in order, the next seeds go back along them, then forward again. That keeps the groups as even as they can be. Here are {{fact:groups-into-knockout.teams}} football teams, listed best seed first: {{fact:groups-into-knockout.signUpOrder}}. They make {{fact:groups-into-knockout.groupCount}} groups of {{fact:groups-into-knockout.groupSize}}, and each group plays a round robin, which [the round-robin schedule post](/blog/round-robin-schedule) explains.

## Who goes through, and in what order

The top {{fact:groups-into-knockout.advance}} of each group go through, which is {{fact:groups-into-knockout.qualifiers}} teams. They are seated in order: the winners of every group first, then the runners-up. Within a place the seats follow the order of the groups, not the records: {{fact:groups-into-knockout.winnersByGroup}} for the winners, then {{fact:groups-into-knockout.runnersByGroup}} for the runners-up. Teams from different groups have never met, so there is no match to put one ahead of another. The one exception is level teams from neighbouring groups: two teams of the same place, next to each other in group order, that are exactly level on the sport's own table rules. A draw number, which the organiser can see and the note further down quotes, puts those in order. The picture marks the teams in each group.

The records in this example rank the other way. Compared across the groups by points and then goal difference, the winners are {{fact:groups-into-knockout.winnersByRecord}} and the runners-up are {{fact:groups-into-knockout.runnersByRecord}}. The seats ignore that. {{fact:groups-into-knockout.bestWinner}} has the best record of the winners and takes seat {{fact:groups-into-knockout.bestWinnerSeat}}, so the byes below go to {{fact:groups-into-knockout.straightThrough}}.

## The bracket

A knockout holds a power of two places. {{fact:groups-into-knockout.qualifiers}} teams in {{fact:groups-into-knockout.bracketSize}} places leave {{fact:groups-into-knockout.byes}} empty, and the empty places go to the best seeds, who are straight through to the next round: {{fact:groups-into-knockout.straightThrough}}. In each match the two seeds add up to one more than the number of places, so the best seed meets the last place, the next best the one before it, and so on. The best of the rest meets the worst of them. Winners mostly meet runners-up from other groups: here {{fact:groups-into-knockout.winnerMatch}} is a winner against a runner-up, and with byes in the bracket {{fact:groups-into-knockout.runnerMatch}} is two runners-up.

## Keeping a group apart in round one

In group order alone, round one would pit {{fact:groups-into-knockout.tableOrderMatch}}, two teams from {{fact:groups-into-knockout.tableOrderClashGroup}}. They already met in the group, and a knockout should not open with a rematch. So the seats are arranged. The group winners keep theirs. The runners-up are tried in seat order, and the first arrangement in which no group meets itself is the one used. Here {{fact:groups-into-knockout.movedUp}} goes up a seat and {{fact:groups-into-knockout.movedDown}} goes down one, and round one is {{fact:groups-into-knockout.drawnMatches}}.

You see this before it is final. It needs the knockout stage to have been generated first, so that its bracket exists with its places empty. When the group stage is complete, the fixtures page says "{{fact:groups-into-knockout.proposalBadge}}". Under "{{fact:groups-into-knockout.proposalHeading}}" it lists each team with the group place it came from and the seat it takes, and the bracket carries a note: "{{fact:groups-into-knockout.drawNote}}" Nothing is filled in until the organiser presses "{{fact:groups-into-knockout.confirmButton}}". {{fact:groups-into-knockout.confirmFinal}}

Often group order is already enough. With four groups and two through from each, round one is {{fact:groups-into-knockout.fourGroupsRoundOne}}. The letter is the group, and the number is the place in it: A1 is the winner of group A and B2 is the runner-up of group B. No group meets itself, so nothing moves.

If no arrangement keeps every group apart, Seazn does not seat the bracket, and says so. It never draws a first round that pairs a group with itself.

## In every sport

Groups into a knockout are offered in all {{fact:groups-into-knockout.sportCount}} sports. Each sport ranks the teams in a group by its own table rules, so what changes from sport to sport is who finishes where, and never how the groups feed the bracket.
