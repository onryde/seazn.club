It depends on the sport. When a league match is level and the rules play on, the win can count as a full win, as a smaller win that still pays the loser something, or as a draw with a bonus for the winner. The table below shows what a new division of each of those sports pays.

## Reading the table

Each row is one way of settling a level match. The first two number columns are what the winner and the loser get. The last two say how the table books the match, and how many points the match hands out between the two sides.

Look at football first. A win in extra time is a win like any other: the winner gets **{{fact:shootout-points.footballOtWin}}** and the loser **{{fact:shootout-points.footballOtLoss}}**. A penalty shoot-out is different. The table books the match as a draw for both sides, and the side that wins the shoot-out gets a bonus point on top, so the winner gets **{{fact:shootout-points.footballSoWin}}** and the loser **{{fact:shootout-points.footballSoLoss}}**.

## The total stays the same

In every row, the two sides together earn what a win in normal time hands out between them: {{fact:shootout-points.footballRegTotal}} in football, {{fact:shootout-points.icehockeyRegTotal}} in IIHF ice hockey, {{fact:shootout-points.icehockeyRecreationalRegTotal}} in recreational ice hockey, {{fact:shootout-points.hockeyRegTotal}} in hockey and {{fact:shootout-points.cricketRegTotal}} in cricket. Playing on never makes a match worth more points than a win in normal time. It changes how the same total is split between the two sides, and the last column of the table shows it.

## What changes from sport to sport

- **Football.** Extra time pays a full win, {{fact:shootout-points.footballOtWin}} to {{fact:shootout-points.footballOtLoss}}. A penalty shoot-out pays {{fact:shootout-points.footballSoWin}} to {{fact:shootout-points.footballSoLoss}}, booked as a draw with a bonus point. A stage plays extra time or a shoot-out only when its match rules have that decider switched on.
- **Ice hockey.** In the IIHF variant an overtime win and a shoot-out win both pay {{fact:shootout-points.icehockeyOtWin}} to the winner and {{fact:shootout-points.icehockeyOtLoss}} to the loser. In the recreational variant an overtime or shoot-out win pays {{fact:shootout-points.icehockeyRecreationalOtWin}} and the loser gets {{fact:shootout-points.icehockeyRecreationalOtLoss}}.
- **Hockey.** With the shoot-out switched on, a level match goes to a shoot-out, and the table books it as a draw for both sides with a bonus point for the winner: {{fact:shootout-points.hockeySoWin}} to {{fact:shootout-points.hockeySoLoss}}, {{fact:shootout-points.hockeySoTotal}} in all.
- **Cricket.** With the super over switched on, a tied match goes to a super over, and the winner of the super over gets {{fact:shootout-points.cricketSoWin}} while the other side gets {{fact:shootout-points.cricketSoLoss}}.
- **{{fact:shootout-points.regulationOnlyList}}.** None of these settles a league match beyond normal time, so there is no overtime or shoot-out win to pay. A match ends when it ends.

## Where you set it

The decider is a match rule, and what each result pays is a points rule. Both sit on a stage's card in the division's Fixtures tab: **Match rules** has the section for deciders, such as a shoot-out or an overtime, and **Points** lists a line for each result the sport can have. The picture shows hockey's Points editor, which spells out that a shoot-out counts as a draw and that the winner gets a bonus point.
