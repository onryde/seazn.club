In a single knockout, one bad match ends your event. Double elimination gives every player a second life: you are out only after your second defeat, unless you forfeit, withdraw or retire, which puts you out at once. A first defeat moves you from the winners bracket to the losers bracket, and the two bracket champions meet in the grand final. A grand final reset keeps the promise in the final too, so the player who has never lost must also be beaten twice.

In Seazn today, a new double elimination is set up without the reset, so the grand final is one match. The example below plays the bracket with the reset on, to show what the reset does, and the post says where it differs.

## The two brackets

Everyone starts in the winners bracket, which works like an ordinary knockout. A player who loses a match there is not out. They drop into the losers bracket and keep playing. A player who loses in the losers bracket has now lost twice, and is out.

One case does not drop: a player who loses by forfeit, withdrawal or retirement is out at once, whichever bracket they are in. They are not sent to the losers bracket, and their opponent goes through.

The losers bracket takes in the players dropping out of each winners round as they arrive, and its survivors play each other in between. Its champion has lost once. The winners bracket champion has not lost at all. They meet in the grand final.

## Why the grand final can need a reset

The grand final is not an even contest of records. The winners bracket champion arrives unbeaten, and the losers bracket champion arrives with one defeat.

- If the winners bracket champion wins, it is over: the other player has their second defeat.
- If the losers bracket champion wins, both players now have one defeat. Ending there would knock out a player after a single loss, which is exactly what double elimination promises not to do. So the two play again: the reset. Whoever wins it is the champion.

## Where to find it in Seazn

Pick **Double elimination** as the division's format. The bracket shows the winners bracket, the losers bracket and the grand final. A player's second defeat puts them out, and so does a forfeit, a withdrawal or a retirement, at the first defeat. If the reset is on and the winners bracket champion wins the first grand final, the reset is not needed and is left unplayed.

As said at the top, a new double elimination is set up without the reset, so a defeat in the grand final is final. The reset is not yet a setting you can switch on in the division builder, so the reset path in the example is how the bracket behaves when the reset is on, not what a new division does today.
