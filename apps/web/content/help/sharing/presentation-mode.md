**Present** is a public board for a venue TV or a projector. It cycles standings, results and the next matches, and needs no sign-in. Open the address on the TV, or cast it, and leave it.

![The Present board for a division, with a Back link, the title and a clock at the top, a standings table of four entrants and the counter 1 / 3.](/help/sharing/present-board.webp "The Present board on its standings slide.")

## Open it

- On a division's public page, select **Present ▸**. It shows on phones and desktops.
- On a competition's public page, select **Present ▸** next to the share controls. It is hidden on a phone's top row. On a phone, open the **Info** tab and use its link.
- Or go straight to `/live/<club>/<competition>/present`. Add `/<division>` for one division.

The board has a **Back** link, the title and a clock.

## What it shows

Slides cycle about every 9 seconds: the standings, **Latest results** and **Coming up**, a bracket slide for a knockout stage, and **In play** while matches are live. Sponsors do not appear on it. On a screen narrower than 1024 px, a phone or a tablet, the board reads **This board is made for a TV**, with **Open the live page** and **Show the board anyway**.

The board wears your public branding: on Pro your brand colour tints it. It is written in your organisation's **Public language**. See [languages](/help/sharing/languages).

## Who can open it

It follows the competition's visibility. A **Link only** or **Public** competition opens for anyone with the address. A **Private** competition shows a not-found page, even to a signed-in member. A **Link only** or **Public** board is never indexed by search engines. See [who can see what](/help/sharing/visibility).

For the signed-in board with your sponsors strip, use [the TV slideshow](/help/sharing/slideshow).

> **Example**
> Your competition is **Link only**. You open `/live/oakfield/spring-open/present` on the hall TV with no sign-in. It shows the standings, then **Latest results**, then **Coming up**, each for about 9 seconds.
