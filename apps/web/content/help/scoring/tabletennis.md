Table tennis divisions score rally by rally: tap the half of the side that won the rally and the point is recorded. Pick a format when you create the division — **Bo5** (games to 11, win by two, no cap — deuce runs 12-10, 15-13 and on), **Bo7** for knockouts and finals, or **Hardbat 21** (games to 21) for legacy and social play.

## The board is the button

Each half shows its points in the current game and the player or pair from the team sheet. Tap the winner's half — *Tap to award the rally* — and that's the entry.

The line above says *Best of 5 · Game 2*, and adds **Deuce** once both sides are one point short of the target and stay level — at 10-all in a game to 11, and at every tie after it, because win-by-two stays in force. A division that sets a hard cap sees **Golden point** there instead.

## Serving, and the serve you're on

The strip under the scores carries **Games** (the match score in games), **Serving**, and **1st serve** / **2nd serve** — which serve of the current turn comes next.

Table tennis serves two points each, so once the app knows who started a game it can work out the rest from the score alone, including the switch to one point each at deuce. In doubles it also names the *player* serving, from the service order on the team sheet.

What it can't do is guess who served first. Until it knows, **Note the server** appears as its own tile: pick who served and who won one rally, and the rotation resolves from there. The tile disappears the moment the app can answer, and never interrupts an ordinary scoring tap.

## Who won the point

Singles credits the side's only player as you tap. Doubles holds the entry for a moment and asks **Which player won it?** — answer it or let it go; the rally counts either way. See [crediting the player](/help/scoring/attribution).

## Time-outs and sanctions

**Time-out** is a tile per side, one tap, no questions — the side is decided by which tile you press.

**Sanction** is also per side. The umpire has exactly two cards, so the ladder offered here is **Warning** (yellow) and **Penalty** (red) and nothing else: expulsion and disqualification are the referee removing a player, not a card an umpire shows, so they're not on the umpire's pad. Then who, asked only in doubles.

## The expedite system

**Start expedite** records the umpire bringing in the expedite system. It's offered only while it could legally be introduced — it disappears once it's already running (it stays in force to the end of the match) and once both sides have reached 9 points in the current game, which is the point at which the rule says it isn't needed.

The pad can't watch the ten-minute clock for you; that half of the rule is still yours to judge.

While expedite is running, the strip shows **Expedite**, and each rally offers a **13th return** chip after the tap — the receiver taking the point on their thirteenth good return. It's offered only when the fold would accept it: if the app knows who served and the *server* won that rally, the question isn't asked at all, because a thirteen-return rally can't credit the server.

## A game's score, without the rallies

**Set score** records a finished game's points directly — two numbers, prefilled from where the current game stands. It's offered only while the current game has no rally recorded, so it returns as each new game opens.

## Recording level

Rally by rally is the finest level table tennis records, and it is available on every plan — a table tennis pad opens there. Drop to a lower level on the **Recording** chip and the halves stop being tappable, because a rally is no longer something this pad records; use **Set score** for each game's final score instead. Set score works at every level; time-outs and sanctions from **Key moments** upwards. See [choosing a detail level](/help/scoring/fidelity).

## Common questions

**Why is there no substitution?** The pair named on the sheet plays the match — table tennis has no substitute, so the pad offers none.

**I recorded the wrong server.** Undo the entry from the activity feed and record it again — see [fixing a mistake](/help/scoring/corrections). The app deliberately won't re-anchor the rotation from a second declaration inside the same game; the next game settles it.

**Team ties (four or five individual matches)?** Each individual match is its own fixture with its own pad. The tie is put together at the division level, not inside one pad.
