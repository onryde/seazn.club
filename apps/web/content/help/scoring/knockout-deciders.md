A knockout match cannot end level, or nobody would go through to the next round. For a match that finishes level, the sport can have a **decider** such as extra time or a shoot-out. A new knockout stage starts with its sport's deciders **on**. You change them in the stage's **Match rules**.

## Which sports have one

| Sport | Decider in a knockout, and where it is set |
|---|---|
| Football | **Extra time**, **Penalty shootout** and the number of kicks per side, in Match rules |
| Cricket | Limited-overs cricket (T20, ODI, The Hundred): **Super over on a tie** and **If the super over is tied**. A drawn Test has no decider, so a level Test in a knockout is held |
| Field hockey | **Shoot-out on a draw** |
| Ice hockey | **Sudden-death overtime** and **Shootout (GWS)** |
| Chess and other board games | **Tie-break games, in order** |
| Carrom | An extra board is always played. It is not an option: **Game tied at max boards** only applies in a league or group stage |
| Generic scoring | None. A level result in a knockout is refused |
| Tennis, badminton, table tennis, volleyball | None. These sports cannot finish level |

The setting is on the stage, not the division. A new knockout stage starts with its sport's deciders on. A league or group stage does not turn them on by itself and draws as before, though the same extra time, shoot-out and super over settings are there if you want them in a table.

## What you see when a level match meets a decider

> **Example**
> A football knockout, with the stage's defaults. Hawks and Rockets are 1-1 at full time. The pad moves into extra time. If it is still level after that, the pad goes to the shoot-out, and the kicks decide the winner.

## When there is no decider

With every decider off, a level match in a knockout is **held**: the fixture shows **Needs a decision**, and **Finalize** is refused with "Settle the match or record its decider before finalizing — a knockout match can't end level." Open the match and press **Settle the match** to pick who goes through. Generic scoring works the same way: a level result in a knockout is refused with "Enter the winner — a knockout match can't end level."

If a match cannot be finished at all, use the forfeit flow for a walkover. An abandoned knockout match is held until you settle it. See [if a match is abandoned](/help/scoring/basics#if-a-match-is-abandoned).

## A shoot-out in a table

A league or group stage can also play a shoot-out when a match ends level. How the table counts it depends on the sport.

- **Football and field hockey:** a draw for both sides, plus a bonus point for the side that won on kicks. The bonus is 1 by default, so with a draw worth 1 the winner gets 2 and the loser 1.
- **Ice hockey, IIHF variant:** a regulation win is worth 3, an overtime or shootout win 2 and an overtime or shootout loss 1. A level match that goes to overtime or a shootout is not booked as a draw. **Recreational variant:** it has no overtime or shootout, a win is worth 2, a draw 1 and a loss 0, and a level match stays a draw.
- **Cricket:** the super over pays as a win and a loss.

See [scoring football](/help/sports/football) for how a shoot-out plays on the pad, and [scoring cricket](/help/sports/cricket) for what the division's still-tied rule does when the super over is also tied.
