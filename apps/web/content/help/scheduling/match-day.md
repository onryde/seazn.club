**Match Day** is the page you keep open while the matches are on. It lists every court and lets you call the next match onto a free one. A division that plays its chess online adds its rounds and boards under the courts.

![Match Day on a phone-width screen with six courts. One match is playing with No scorer, one court is Called, three are Free (one with a match queued and two folded into a line), and one is Closed for the rest of today.](/help/scheduling/match-day.webp "Match Day with a match playing, a court called, a free court and a closed court.")

## Open it

On a day with matches, the competition page shows **Open Match Day**. On other days it is the first item in the **Open** menu, as **Match Day**. On a phone the **Open** menu is not there, so it is under **More**.

Owners and admins run the day. A Viewer in your organisation opens the same page and sees **View only. Calling matches and scorer links are for the competition's organisers.** They cannot call a match, make a scorer link or make a Match Day code. Anyone outside your organisation gets a page-not-found.

## Read the court list

The header counts what is on: for example **6 courts**, **1 playing**, **3 free**. Each court row reads **Playing** with its match, **Called** while a match waits for its players, **Free**, or **Closed** with the hours it is shut. **In use: Other Cup** means a match from another competition is on the court, and you can't use it until that match ends.

Free courts with nothing queued fold into a line such as **2 free courts**. Select a court to see its scoreboard and **Up next here**.

## Call a match to a court

1. Select a free court. It lists the matches that could start there in three groups: **Due here next**, **Waiting elsewhere** and **No court yet**.
2. Select **Call to** and the court name on the match you want. A sheet opens.
3. Read the sheet, then select **Call to** again to confirm.

The sheet shows each later match on that court with its planned time and the time it is now expected, marked with a **~**: **planned 19:29, now ~19:49**. It ends with **Players aren't notified. Tell them where to go.**

After you confirm, the court shows **Called: waiting for players** and **Called at** with the time.

A call moves the match you called, and nothing else. It takes the court you chose and the time of the call, and the public schedule shows it there. Every other match keeps its planned time. The **~** times are estimates you see only on this page.

Select **Undo call** to put the match back on its old court at its old time. Once the match has started, or you have moved it on the board since the call, Undo no longer works.

A match whose player is already on another court is greyed out with the reason, for example **Birch Lane is on Court 1**. A court with a match playing or called takes no call.

> **Example**
> Court 2 is free and reads **3 matches could start here now**. Four more are greyed out because a player is on Court 1. You call the cup semi-final Garnet v Fenwick, planned for 20:34 on Court 1. The sheet shows Birch Lane v Cedar Hill, planned 19:29, now **~19:49**, and **1 match starts later than planned.** You confirm at 19:19. Court 2 shows **Called at 19:19** and the semi-final's own time becomes 19:19. Every other match keeps its planned time. **Undo call** puts the semi-final back on Court 1 at 20:34.

## Closed courts and warnings

A court shows **Closed** when its own opening hours say it is shut: **Closed for the rest of today**, or a range such as **Closed 19:36–20:06**. You set opening hours and closed dates on each court. You can still call a match onto a closed court. The sheet asks first, for example **Court 4 is closed for the rest of today. Call anyway?** and the button reads **Call anyway**.

A warning never stops a call. The sheet warns you when:

- Later matches start late: **1 match starts later than planned.**
- The match would still be on when the court closes: **Court 3 closes from 19:36 to 20:06, while this match would still be on.**
- A player's rest is shorter than the division asks for.
- The court's matches would run past its closing time.

**Divisions** narrows the page to some divisions' matches. **Venues** does the same for venues and appears only when your courts are in more than one venue. Neither stops a call.

## Scorers, codes and streams

A playing court shows **No scorer** while nobody has started scoring it, no official is assigned to it and it has no scoring link. To give a scorer a link, or to make a Match Day code for the Seazn Club app, see [scorers and Match Day codes](/help/scheduling/match-day-scorers). To stream every match on a court from one phone, see [streaming a court](/help/scheduling/match-day-stream).

## Online chess rounds and boards

A division that plays its chess online adds an **Online** block under the courts. Each round has a row with its state: **closes 19:58** over **9 of 12 here** while players check in, then for example **3 of 6 playing**, and at the ends **Not started**, **Paired, not opened** and **Finished**. Its boards are listed under it, each with its own state, such as **Checking in**, **Live · move 12**, **On hold** or the result. **Boards you are playing on are not listed here.**

Select a round or a board to open its panel next to the list. On a phone the panel fills the screen, and **Back to the list** takes you back.

- The round panel follows the round. Once the division has started and the round is paired, it lists the pairings and, for an organiser, has **Open round**. A confirmation says how many players it invites and that their clocks start now. While players check in the panel shows the countdown, how many have checked in and **Waiting on 2 boards**, with **Resend invite** and **+5 min (3 left)** on each row (see [adding time to check-in](/help/sports/chess)). When every board of a Swiss round is over it offers **Pair next round**, and once the next round is paired it says **Round 4 is ready to open**.
- The board panel shows who plays White and Black, where the game stands, the flags raised on the board and, for a board on hold, why it is held. Under **Your decision** it offers what applies to the board: **Resync from Lichess**, **Let the game go on** while it has flags, and **Settle the result now…**. During a video outage a card offers to let the board play without video.
- Cameras: when the division asks for cameras, the board panel shows one tile for each player, with the video or a line saying why there is none. **Hide video** folds the tiles to one line each and **Show video** brings them back. A tile that could not load has **Try again**. On a narrow screen a **Cameras** and **Board** switch picks which of the two shows.

A held board or a board with a report also appears in the **Needs you** list behind the header button, with an action such as **Review board 4** that opens its panel. A Viewer sees the rounds and boards and has no buttons that change anything.

## Common questions

**Why can't I call a match?** Either the page says **View only** because your role is Viewer, or your organisation is over its plan's limit on active competitions. Then matches still score, but you can't call or undo calls here, or connect a new stream. Archive a competition or see [plans](/help/billing/plans).

**Where is the Needs you list?** It is behind a button in the Match Day header, such as **3 need you**. The competition page keeps one line that links to it.
