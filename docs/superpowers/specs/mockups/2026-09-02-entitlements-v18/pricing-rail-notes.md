# The sport rail, and Pro on the plate

**The rail is a board, not a pill row.** A box office prints its running order on a board behind the counter, so the ten sports sit in hairline-ruled slots (10 across at 1280, 5×2 at 768) under the promise line that claims them — with no eyebrow label, because that sentence already is the label and one more tracked-caps tag would have been the page's fourth. Quiet by *case and value*, not just size: sentence-case Barlow Condensed against the marquee's shouting caps, so it informs without out-arguing the ticket. The catch-all is deliberately not an eleventh sport — other face, lower case, full width, the board's foot line. No count is printed.

**Elevating Pro (`-pro`).** One structural move: Pro takes the devices the Pass was spending on a one-time $15 — the lit night plate, the page's only *filled* lime button, the biggest number after the headline — and goes last, after Free, which drops from a column to a low flat strip. The Pass keeps everything that makes it a ticket (full width, cream on night, stub, tear, serial, ADMIT ONE, two rungs) and stays the largest object; its button becomes outlined lime. Contact drops to a hairline band so it cannot blunt the landing. Ranked by contrast, not hue, so greyscale holds: light field (Pass) → flat light strip (Free) → darkest field, whitest numerals, the one solid button (Pro).

## What is different at 320 and why

- **Portrait ticket, not the landscape one narrowed.** A CSS grid switches composition at 768. On a phone the promise leads, the stub runs *across the top* carrying the serial and ADMIT ONE horizontally along the short edge, the tear is horizontal, the counterfoil follows. At 1280 the same anatomy turns landscape — stub down the right, tear vertical, ADMIT ONE up the edge.
- **Order changes with width.** Desktop: eyebrow → promise → bullets → price. Phone: promise → price → buy, *then* the tear, then bullets. The decision sits on the ticket's first screen, not after nine bullets. Same reasoning puts Pro's price, promise and button before a single bullet in `-pro`.
- **The buy action repeats at the ticket's foot, phone only** (`md:hidden`). The mobile ticket is a screen and a half tall; the repeat returns the action to the thumb at the point of decision. Pointless at desktop, where the stub button never leaves the screen.
- **M and L stay two-up at 320** — deliberate, not inherited. $15 and $29 are a *comparison*, and two columns is how a comparison reads; stacking makes it a sequence and pushes the buy button ~110px further down. The detail lines wrap to three short lines: that is the price of keeping the two numbers side by side, and it is worth paying.
- **The rail becomes a different device.** Ten ruled slots two-up would run five rows deep and shove the ticket off the first screen, so at 320 it collapses to a printed ribbon — names flowing inline with hairline dividers, wrapping over three lines in about a third of the height. Slots only appear once there is width to rule them.
- **Type set for a phone, not scaled down.** Bullets 15.2px (14.4 desktop), rung prices 42px, buttons 16px on 56px targets, ribbon names 16px (15 desktop). Muted purples lifted for an outdoor read (#8d7fc0 → #9a8cc9, #b7aede → #bdb4e2).
- **Same at both widths, on purpose:** the h1, the marquee lamp, and the tear/scallop vocabulary. Already phone-scaled — and the ticket metaphor is the one thing that must not change between the two compositions, or it stops being the same product.
