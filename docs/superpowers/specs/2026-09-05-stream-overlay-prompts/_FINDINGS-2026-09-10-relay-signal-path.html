Tiers

Three products, one render path

The invariant is load-bearing rather than tidy: because both graphics tiers consume the same route, a club moving between them sees identical output. That is also why the browser-as-compositor shape (B3) was right and not merely what x11grab forced — the composited tier is you running the club's OBS browser source for them.

The tiers are different customers, not different quality. Tier A needs a laptop, OBS, an operator and a wired uplink, and in exchange gives the club multi-camera switching, scenes, audio mixing and replays natively. Tier B needs a phone and nobody. A club with kit gets a better production out of Tier A than the compositor will ever hand them.

delayMs is a Tier B concept only. In Tier A the picture is local and instant while the score arrives over realtime, so the score trails play harmlessly. In Tier B the picture is delayed by the whole SRT → Cloudflare → WHEP chain while the score stays realtime-fast, so without the seam it would run ahead of play and spoil a wicket. Tier A's equivalent trap is Q3: a club holding streaming.overlay without realtime polls at fifteen seconds, with no compositor in between to mask it.

A fourth shape exists that no tier covers: the phone publishes to Cloudflare and the club's OBS pulls it back over SRT or RTMPS playback, compositing locally. That serves a club with a laptop that wants the phone as its camera, and it costs no Machine.

Path

Inside the composited tier

The invariant holds because the video lives inside the page: Chromium renders /overlay/fixtures/[id]/relay with the WHEP element under an unmodified OverlayStage, and x11grab takes the composite. Every score pixel on every tier comes from one route. That shape is also forced — x11grab is RGB only, so overlay capture with alpha was never available.

The encode line is binding verbatim:

-f x11grab -framerate 30 -i :99 -f pulse -i default -c:v libx264 -preset veryfast
-b:v 3000k -maxrate 3000k -bufsize 6000k -g 60 -bf 2 -pix_fmt yuv420p
-c:a aac -b:a 128k -ar 48000 -f flv <rtmps>

The Machine is created per session over the Machines REST API in lhr and never moves: Cloudflare's ingest is anycast, so the phone already reaches a nearby PoP and relocating the compositor buys nothing.

Failure

Why the encoder never restarts

This is the property the front door exists for. The phone drops, Cloudflare holds the input, the video element goes stalled, the page swaps to the slate at eight seconds, and ffmpeg keeps encoding the same X display at the same 30 fps throughout. Nothing downstream learns that the camera went away.

It also explains the supporting reasons in their real order of weight: anycast ingest puts the first hop near the phone rather than in London; the passthrough tier can exist at all because it needs no Machine; Machine lifecycle decouples from the phone, so a retry is invisible; and a public listener with a routable port, auth and DoS exposure is a service Cloudflare already operates.

Two things this property is not free of. It rests on an undocumented Cloudflare behaviour (U1), and “the encoder never restarts” is a property of the compositor rather than of Cloudflare — plain ffmpeg exits on input EOF, so the slate has to live on the input side of the capture, which the browser-as-compositor shape gives you by construction.

Audio

Sound takes the same trip as the picture

phone mic → AAC → SRT/RTMPS → Cloudflare → Opus (WHEP)
  → Chromium decodes to PCM → PulseAudio null sink
  → -f pulse -i default → AAC 128k / 48 kHz → FLV → YouTube

There is no separate audio path and no passthrough: the sound is decoded inside the browser and re-encoded by the same ffmpeg process that encodes the screen. Two consequences are good and two are findings.

Silence is continuous, which is the point

A null sink emits samples whether or not anything is playing. During a phone drop the audio input never starves and the muxer holds, so the connection stays healthy on both tracks. This is the audio half of “the encoder never restarts”, and it works by construction rather than by handling.

One A/V clock is a correct claim

A null sink has no hardware clock — it runs on the system timer, the same reference x11grab polls against. The two captures cannot drift apart the way two physical devices would. The clap burst in the soak is the right signal to prove it; it just has no assertion reading it (T2).

Two lossy generations in transit

AAC on the phone, Opus for WebRTC, AAC on the way out. At 128k for crowd noise and commentary this is inaudible, but it is the reason not to skimp on the phone's audio bitrate — nothing downstream can recover what the first encode discards. There is also no normalisation anywhere in the chain, so a hot or quiet microphone reaches YouTube as-is.

Two independent ways to ship silence

D1 and D2 both produce a stream with an audio track containing nothing, from different causes. Fix them together: if only one is fixed, the level assertion added for T1 still fails and it will read as the first fix not working.

Protocol

SRT or RTMPS on the first hop

Cloudflare's live input exposes both endpoints, so this choice touches the first hop only — the hold, the WHEP pull, the compositor and the YouTube leg are identical either way.

The constraint dissolved when the plugin did. The reason to consider RTMPS-only was that HaishinKit.kt ships no SRT module, so a third-party Flutter plugin could not reach SRT on Android at any price. Owning the binding over StreamPack removes that: streampack-srt and HaishinKit.swift's SRTHaishinKit both ship SRT, so both protocols are reachable on both platforms from day one. The extra cost is roughly a dependency and an endpoint class per platform.

What SRT buys here is not latency. YouTube's own 15–40 s dominates the budget, so nothing in this chain is latency-constrained. SRT buys loss recovery on the cellular uplink at a wet ground, which is where the drops actually come from. RTMP is TCP: under sustained loss it retransmits, collapses its congestion window, backs the encoder's send buffer up and eventually disconnects — with no latency dial to tune.

Recommendation: SRT primary, RTMPS automatic fallback. SRT is UDP, and some venue wifi and corporate networks drop it, so SRT-only is fragile in exactly the places a club will stream from. Try SRT with a generous buffer (2000 ms — you have the headroom), fall back to RTMPS on failure. It mirrors the WHEP→LL-HLS ladder the relay page already runs, and both engines support both, so the fallback is configuration rather than architecture.

This makes C1 mandatory rather than defensive — the capture credentials now genuinely carry two shapes — and largely settles T5, since the soak's SRT leg becomes the production primary.

Multi-cam

What several cameras would cost

The compositor does not change. The relay page holds N video elements instead of one — switcher, picture-in-picture, a 2×2 tile — and it is all DOM and CSS. x11grab still grabs one 1280×720 screen, one ffmpeg, one Machine, one RTMPS out. A keyed-overlay compositor would have needed a real vision mixer here; B3 needs a layout change.

Per-source delay has a proper mechanism

A live video element cannot be held back arbitrarily, but WebRTC exposes the right knob: RTCRtpReceiver.jitterBufferTarget (Chrome's playoutDelayHint) raises a receiver's target playout delay in milliseconds. Measure each source's measuredLatencyMs, set every receiver to max(latency) + margin so all sources present at one common wall-clock time, then hold the score against that time with the single delayMs R2 already builds. The operator nudge layers on per source, and D3's snap-on-transport-change rule applies per source too.

Cross-source sync needs NTP, and has a floor without it

Two phones have independent clocks and no genlock. Even with transport latency perfectly equalised their capture instants differ, so a cut mid-delivery jumps. Broadcast solves this with PTP; handsets cannot. The workable answer is for each phone to report an NTP-synced capture timestamp through the session API, folded into that source's playout target — handset NTP lands within tens of milliseconds, which reads clean on a cut. Without it, accept 100–300 ms of cross-source error and never cut during a delivery.

Switching rides a channel you already have

The relay page subscribes to Supabase realtime for scores. A camera cut can ride the same channel — sub-second, no new transport, and it lands as fast as a goal does. The 15 s heartbeat is far too slow to carry a switch, and does not need to.

The rest is logged as M1–M4. The short version: R2 should ship single-camera and prove the soak, but the two artefacts already open — the QR contract and the session data model — are far cheaper to shape one-to-many now than to migrate later.

Register

Findings and gaps

Twenty-two items against R2's prompt, §7 of the design, the capture app and multi-camera. Grouped by kind. Nothing in D–C moves R2's shape; P belongs to R3 and M to a wave not yet written, both listed here because several of their rows constrain artefacts open right now. Each is a recommendation, not a ruling.

A muted media element sends nothing to the audio device, so -f pulse -i default encodes silence. §7.3 never specifies muted and §7.2 calls the null sink “one A/V clock” — the attribute is prompt-versus-design drift, and --autoplay-policy=no-user-gesture-required is already set, so it is not buying autoplay either.

D3

The delayMs ramp cannot cross a WHEP→LL-HLS transition

The ladder falls back after 5 s, roughly a 0.5 s→6 s step. At ≤ 250 ms per 15 s beat that is 22 beats. The score runs ahead of the picture for about five and a half minutes, at exactly the moment the transport degrades. The ramp is right for drift and wrong for a discontinuity — snap, then resume EWMA.

~5.5 min
D4

The Machine restart policy is not pinned to no

With any other policy a non-zero exit restarts the Machine in place and R1's sweep creates a replacement. Two compositors, one session, one stream key — YouTube receiving two encoders is visible corruption rather than a clean failure, and the soak's Machine-kill check may pass straight through it.

D5

x11grab samples on a clock Chromium does not share

The grabber polls the framebuffer at 30 fps while Chromium paints on its own schedule under SwiftShader. The two clocks beat, producing periodic duplicate and dropped frames. Judder shows on pans, which football and cricket cameras do continuously. R0 watch 1 asks whether capture works at 720p, which is a question about capture, not smoothness.

T5

The soak covers the primary leg but not the fallback

Scope 7 drives synthetic SRT and asserts “a mid-match SRT kill → slate ≤ 10 s → seamless resume”, which matches production now that SRT is primary. What it does not cover is the RTMPS fallback path, whose failure shape is different — TCP stalls and backs up before it disconnects, so the slate fires later and less cleanly than an SRT drop. Add a fallback run, or accept that the fallback's slate timing is unproven.

U1 · load-bearing and undocumented
U1

Nobody knows what the playback side sees during the hold

Cloudflare documents timeoutSeconds as governing when a disconnect creates a new video, and the Stream Live write-up describes a volatile/committed state split so broadcasters can resume after a blip. Neither says whether the WHEP or RTMPS playback connection stays open and starves, or closes. The whole composited tier's no-restart property rests on this.

Ten-minute experiment: attach a player to the playback URL, kill the encoder, watch thirty seconds. If the pull closes, the slate must be driven by reconnect logic rather than by frame starvation — same outcome, different code, and much harder to retrofit once R2 has hardened.

Contract

C1–C2 · contract and port shape
C1

QR contract v1 cannot express two credential shapes

§7.6 fixes it as “the only R3 fact fixed here”. SRT is three fields; RTMPS is two. With SRT primary and RTMPS as the automatic fallback the app needs both sets in hand at scan time — it cannot re-scan a QR code when UDP turns out to be blocked at the ground. So this is no longer a defensive discriminator: v1 must carry both credential sets and say which is preferred, or the fallback cannot happen without a round trip the phone may not have connectivity for.

§7.6 · docs/contracts/capture-qr.v1.json

Blocks fallback
C2

The front-door port hides the endpoint but not the semantics

Keeping the credential fields opaque so “a front-door swap never breaks deployed phones” is right. But “holds the input for N seconds after disconnect” is now a behavioural clause in that port's contract. A swap to a front door without a hold would silently delete the no-restart property while every field still validates and every deployed phone still connects. Write the window into the interface definition, not just the fields.

§7.1 RunnerProvider · the SRT field triple

Widen the port

Capture app

P1–P5 · R3 scope, constrains R2

The app owns its binding: HaishinKit.swift on iOS, StreamPack on Android, React Native shell. No third-party streaming plugin — haishin_kit was ruled out on its own maintainer's advice and its Android engine has no SRT. These are the risks that survive that decision, and P1 changes what the soak should prove.

P1

iOS drops the camera when backgrounded; Android does not

Android keeps publishing behind a foreground service with the right foregroundServiceType. iOS will not keep the camera running in the background at all. Over three hours the operator's phone will lock, take a call, or get switched away from — so on iOS the slate and reconnect path stops being an edge case and becomes a normal operating mode. Either the app prevents idle lock and warns the operator, or §7.4's choreography runs many times per match.

R3 scope · interacts with §7.4 and the soak

Not write-once
P2

The publish loop must live in native code, not in JavaScript

If the JS layer supervises the session, a stalled JS thread or a backgrounded app takes the stream with it. Native must own connect, publish, reconnect and protocol fallback, reporting upward at about 1 Hz; JS drives the UI and nothing else. This is a design rule rather than a defect, recorded because it is cheap to honour up front and expensive to retrofit — and because it is the reason React Native's performance profile is not a risk to the video path.

R3 native module boundary

Design rule
P3

Thermal and battery behaviour over a full match is unvalidated

Sustained encode plus radio for three hours throttles most handsets long before charge runs out, and no phone streams an ODI on battery regardless. The app should assume mains or a large PD bank, and 720p30 at the current 3000k is the right ceiling — but none of this has been measured on the handsets clubs actually own. Framework-independent: Flutter or native would face the identical curve.

R3 scope · device matrix

Unmeasured
P4

Landscape capture is a known trap in this problem space

The one issue ever filed against HaishinKit.dart was #160, “preview and outgoing stream remain portrait when device is landscape” — which suggests orientation handling is a genuine trap in these engines rather than one plugin's slip. A tripod at a ground shoots landscape, so this is the only orientation that matters. Prove preview orientation, encoded orientation and rotation metadata separately; they fail independently.

R3 scope · precedent: HaishinKit.dart #160

P5

No device spike has been run against the chosen engines

One afternoon before R3 commits: StreamPack on a real Android handset and HaishinKit.swift on a real iPhone, landscape, publishing three hours into a real Cloudflare input, with the screen locking part-way. Settles P1, P3 and P4 together, and gives the binding estimate a floor. Runs in parallel with R2 — the soak drives a synthetic source and needs no phone, so nothing here is on R2's critical path.

Multi-camera

M1–M4 · own wave, two decisions due now

Not an R2 amendment — R2 ships single-camera. But M3 and M4 affect artefacts being written this month, so they cannot wait for the wave that owns them.

M1

N× WHEP decode under SwiftShader has never been benched

R0 sized the Machine for one software-decoded 720p WebRTC stream. Four, plus page compositing, may not fit an economic guest. The broadcast answer is program-plus-preview — keep two receivers hot, the rest cold, and accept 1–2 s of WHEP renegotiation when a third is pulled up. That is a bench question of exactly R0's shape and belongs before any multi-cam spec.

R0 memo scope · §7.2 guest sizing

Bench first
M2

Cross-source capture sync has no mechanism at all

Equalising transport latency with jitterBufferTarget aligns arrival, not capture. Independent handset clocks with no genlock mean a cut mid-delivery jumps. Each phone must report an NTP-synced capture timestamp through the session API, folded into its playout target. Handset NTP is accurate to tens of milliseconds; without it the floor is 100–300 ms of visible error.

R3 capture app · session API · new field

NTP required
M3

The session data model is one-to-one and multi-cam is one-to-many

Today: one session, one live input, one Machine. Multi-cam: one broadcast, N inputs, one Machine. That reshapes the state machine, credits, the Phone tab, and the QR contract — which is already being reopened for C1's dual credentials. Add the slot concept while it is open; migrating a shipped one-to-one model later is far more expensive.

R1 session model · §7.6 · shares a window with C1

Reshape now
M4

Multi-camera may belong to Tier A, not Tier B

OBS already does multi-camera switching, scenes, audio mixing and replay natively and free. A club with a laptop gets a better production from Tier A today than a multi-cam wave would deliver in Tier B. That may be the honest line between the tiers rather than a gap to close — and it is a product ruling, not an engineering one, so it should be taken before anyone estimates M1.
